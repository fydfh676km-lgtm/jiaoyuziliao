# Visual-system decision contract

Use this reference after `content_approved` to determine the visual system and page-by-page blueprint. After the explicit start command, complete this checkpoint internally and continue production; no separate visual approval is required.

Apply this contract within each selected courseware variant under `deliverables.md`. Source understanding and reference inspection may be shared, but keep A/B's selected direction, page concepts and scene/continuity checks version-scoped; do not compare A's last page with B's first as one deck or overwrite A's accepted design with B's decisions. A version-specific reference instruction applies only to that version. Each version still has one coherent visual world and its own complete six-field sequence.

## Choose the visual basis from the user's instruction

This rule supersedes earlier automatic style-research and automatic-library routes.

- **No designated or explicitly requested visual references:** use the already checked original textbook understanding and approved lesson/page content directly. Derive the scene, imagery, emotional tone, viewpoint and visual structure from those materials. Do not open visual-reference searches, retrieve old/bundled images, or ask the user to supply a reference. Textbook screenshots or illustrations provided for reading are source material, not automatically style references. Reuse the existing source understanding rather than rereading all materials at this stage.
- **User explicitly supplies/designates reference images:** open the actual designated images and treat them as the primary authority for style, palette, medium/material, brushwork, atmosphere and light language. Adopt their abstract spatial and focal-hierarchy principles, not their exact scene, subject placement, frame silhouette or text-region geometry as a deck-wide template. Independently conceive each page's scene or knowledge relationship from the understood textbook and that page's teaching purpose, within the reference-led aesthetic. This does not authorize style reselection or a departure from the reference's defining richness. The page copy/order, classroom readability and factual content remain intact. Do not automatically search for additional images or blend in a library.
- **Only an explicit request enables supplementary reference sources:** search the web for visual references only when currently requested; retrieve an authorized persistent/bundled library only when the user asks to use that library or their previously supplied references. A style phrase such as 古风水墨, the genre of ancient poetry, or the mere presence of a stored library is not such a request. Preserve the library assets; do not delete or silently activate them. Any expressly requested supplemental source remains subordinate to the designated primary reference style unless the user asks to replace it.

The style-reference route never authorizes copying a third-party page or changing approved teaching facts. If a reference's subject is factually incompatible, retain its aesthetic language and substitute lesson-correct subjects; do not discard its whole style merely because the textbook suggests another default. Unknown season, place or historical facts remain unknown, not invented to fit an image. Readability and restrained emphasis still apply even if the reference contains tiny or decorative text.

Missing/unreadable designated images are a real input gap, not permission to substitute unrequested network/library images.

Search snippets alone are not image evidence. Use the system default browser if browser interaction is needed, unless another is specified. A frozen image-only rendering contract keeps its supplied basis without reopening planning.

Translate the selected reference language or textbook-derived decisions into every page's existing six fields: style/material, subject/element choices, typographic hierarchy, composition, camera/light and continuity/variation. Images and style keywords are not a substitute for page-specific instructions.

## Reference authenticity and low-AI gate

Before extracting style rules or drafting the blueprint, inspect every supplied or collected reference for realism, provenance, and visible AI artifacts. Separate what is directly observable from any claim that the image is AI-generated; when the distinction cannot be established confidently, label the source `AI-likeness/provenance unverified` and use it only as an abstract style reference. Never copy its factual imagery, wording, logo, watermark, pseudo-text, or composition. If a visual reference has strong AI artifacts, do not reproduce those artifacts in the blueprint or generation prompt.

The visual system and generation prompts should deliberately minimize generic AI appearance. Prefer natural proportions, physically coherent light/shadow, believable material grain, intentional color roles, purposeful asymmetry, authentic editorial/photographic/print/handmade cues, and topic-specific objects. Reject or regenerate assets with waxy/plastic surfaces, incoherent gradients, impossible anatomy or perspective, repetitive cloned details, floating objects, illegible pseudo-text, accidental logos/watermarks, unmotivated symmetry, generic glossy 3D, or unrelated decorative effects. Deliberately vivid color, dense craftsmanship, ornate framing and intricate scenes are legitimate when they belong to the selected visual basis; neither saturation nor detail density alone is an AI defect. Repair the observed defect rather than automatically desaturating, flattening or simplifying the chosen style. Treat this as an internal preflight and asset-quality gate; it does not add a seventh field to the user-facing six-field blueprint.

## Scene-driven prompts and Chinese-aesthetic routing

Give the selected direction a coherent atmosphere and spatial/material language. Each page foregrounds a concrete scene or knowledge relationship tied to its teaching purpose; a style label such as `古风背景`, `水墨风`, `山水画`, or `唯美意境` is not a complete visual instruction. For scene-led pages, define a recognizable place, moment, viewpoint and atmosphere using this content-based recipe:

1. identify the page's teaching purpose and the exact textual image, event, object, person, or relationship that should become the visual focus;
2. define the viewpoint and spatial depth through foreground, middle ground, and distance, using scale, overlap, mist, light, water, paths, architecture, or vegetation only when they fit the source;
3. specify source-supported season, time of day and weather; when these are unknown, retain an unspecified or neutral setting and distinguish chosen light, color temperature, material/brush language and emotional treatment as design decisions rather than textbook facts;
4. reserve purposeful negative space and a readable text region without weakening the main scene; and
5. keep every motif historically, geographically, seasonally, and semantically compatible with the text.

For ancient poetry and classical prose such as `《论语》`, the user's standing preference is poetic atmosphere with concrete, meaningful scenes: illustration should help learners enter the text's situation or understand its ideas. With no current explicit style/reference, favor a coherent Chinese literary visual world combining ink/light-color treatment and classical scene illustration. Blue-green light-color ink, paper tones, layered mist and carefully drawn figures, architecture or objects are useful treatments, chosen for the text rather than a fixed palette or prop list. Retain purposeful detail and spatial depth. A current explicit style or designated reference leads the aesthetic instead; textbook understanding keeps the pictured content accurate. This preference applies to A and B and does not enable visual search, library retrieval or asset generation.

For poetry, build each page around its actual imagery, speaker's situation, viewing position, motion/stillness and emotional movement. Season, geography and time remain source-grounded; changes of framing, light and density can express feeling without asserting invented facts. For `《论语》` and related classical texts, begin with the chapter's question, exchange, action, learning practice or human relationship; use a relevant teaching/life situation where it helps understanding. Keep interpretive staging distinct from a documented event, use only source-supported dialogue, and keep any illustrative situation within the approved lesson. An abstract concept, comparison or relationship page may use a clear visual structure with supporting scene detail; a figure or panoramic landscape is not compulsory on every page.

A multi-poem or multi-text deck may use controlled, source-driven chapter dialects—related ink wash, blue-green landscape, gongbi, mineral color or classical literary illustration—within one master material logic, palette roles, text hierarchy, border grammar and emotional progression. Change the page's focal content, action, viewpoint, depth and information organization with its teaching purpose. A broad opening scene, a context-led human scene, an imagery close-up, a comparison and a concept relationship can have different compositions without becoming different visual styles. The reference's moon, plum branch, pavilion, window, desk or complete background is not a recurring page template. Use the existing nearest-similar-page and interchangeability checks; do not add decorative novelty or another lesson merely to make pages different.

Keep the result vivid without turning it into a pile of generic `Chinese-style` symbols. Seals, scrolls, cloud patterns, cranes, lanterns, bamboo, plum blossom, pagodas, torn paper, and calligraphy are optional content-dependent accents, not an automatic checklist. Remove motifs unrelated to the selected content and visual basis. Build one memorable scene with purposeful, authentic details; its richness may be elaborate or restrained according to the chosen direction, rather than a universal minimalism rule. Avoid tourist-poster clichés and source-incompatible fantasy spectacle.

Encode the scene through the fixed six user-facing fields only:

- `画面风格` begins with a compact master-world anchor, then names any content-driven chapter dialect, medium or brush treatment, atmosphere, and color relationship;
- `添加元素` identifies content-supported subjects and their foreground/middle-ground/distant placement, including only necessary traditional accents;
- `文字字体与重点标注` defines typographic hierarchy, relative scale and weight, placement, color roles, and selective emphasis without naming a specific font by default;
- `布局排版` defines scene-to-text proportion, visual movement, negative space, reading order, and the stable reading zone;
- `镜头与光影` defines camera position and height, shot scale, viewpoint or lens feeling, spatial depth, time, light direction, softness, color temperature, and atmosphere; and
- `画面去重与统一约束` states this page's unique scene signature, how it differs from the nearest similar or adjacent pages, what must not be reused, and which master-world anchors remain constant.

Controlled chapter dialects do not create unrelated decks. Vary the crop, spatial depth, focal subject, accent palette, related brush treatment, and scene rhythm from page to page or text to text while retaining the same master material, palette roles, typographic hierarchy, border/shape grammar, recurring anchors, and emotional logic. When the optional anti-garbled-text mode is active, keep the landscape and classical scene richness, but place required inner-page text in the clean, front-facing, high-contrast reading regions defined by that mode; page 1 retains its existing visual-quality exception.

## Composition, focal hierarchy, continuity, readability, and rhythm

Apply `classroom-readability.md` to every classroom page in both ordinary and anti-garbled-text modes: use explicit standard-widescreen-equivalent size targets in the existing typography field and allocate layout capacity before illustration size. Essential body copy and labels are at least 32 pt; titles at least 40 pt; focal quotations, prompts and conclusions preferably 36–44 pt. Preserve ordinary-mode font-name freedom and the anti-garbled cover typography exception; neither exempts essential text from classroom readability. Do not shrink approved copy or compress spacing to make it fit. These are slide sizes, not the Word document's paragraph sizes.

Before choosing a composition, classify the page's teaching function internally: cover or opening, context, explanation or close reading, comparison or evidence, relationship/map/timeline, activity or practice, synthesis, transfer, or closing. Select a content-appropriate composition archetype from that function. A deck's subject style does not justify forcing every page into the same landscape, desk, scroll, split-screen, card grid, or text-over-image arrangement.

Give every page one primary visual focal point and an intentional reading order. Define what the audience sees first, reads second, and inspects last; use one main subject or core diagram with no more than two supporting semantic groups. A semantic group may contain multiple tightly related objects. Overview, comparison, route, timeline, and task pages may use three to five visible nodes when those nodes belong to one clear organizing structure and do not compete as equal focal points. The title, scene, person, decorative frame, and information graphic must not compete at equal strength. Encode the focal order and reading path inside `布局排版`.

When a person, place, building, or important prop recurs, create an internal recurring-character and recurring-object continuity sheet before drafting the page blueprints. Keep identity anchors stable across pages: age and facial features, hair, clothing silhouette and color, role, era, architecture, vessel, book, desk, map, tool, or other defining form. If the source requires a time, season, location, or emotional change, describe that change while preserving enough anchors for the entity to remain recognizable. Put page-specific continuity cues inside `添加元素`; do not print the continuity sheet as an extra deliverable.

Before drafting the user-facing blueprint, first make a concise visual decision for each page: what concrete scene or knowledge relationship should learners see here, and which source detail and teaching purpose make that choice specific to this page? Put that decision in the existing shared page record, then extend its visual map with page/text or chapter, teaching function, primary subject/action, composition axis or route, shot/viewpoint, time/light/weather, motion or stillness, information structure, nearest similar page, and forbidden reuse. A close observation, evidence comparison or relationship diagram can be the correct visual concept; not every page needs another panoramic landscape. Do not separately rewrite a second full page plan or expose a reasoning transcript. For a multi-text deck, plan a justified emotion-and-light progression. Use these decisions to populate the six output fields; the internal map is not another deliverable.

Make each page description self-contained enough to be copied into an external image-generation call without losing the deck identity. Begin `画面风格` with a short master-world anchor, then add the chapter dialect and page-specific scene. In `添加元素`, name the primary subject, action or state, approximate count or role where relevant, foreground/middle-ground/distant placement, scale, relationships, and any historical/geographic constraints. In `布局排版`, name the dominant axis or path, image-to-text proportion and zones, reading order, and purposeful negative space. Avoid padding these fields with repeated generic prose.

A baseline classroom-readability floor applies even when the optional anti-garbled-text mode is inactive. This baseline does not activate that mode, require large blank panels, prohibit artistic typography, or reduce scene richness. It requires projected body text to remain comfortably legible, adequately contrasted, and separated from high-frequency texture or competing detail; do not describe required body copy as tiny, narrow, miniature, fine-print, or “缩小一级”, do not shrink essential copy to enlarge an illustration, let decoration cross critical character strokes, or use low-contrast thin text. By default, describe typographic character and hierarchy rather than naming a specific installed font or font category; an explicit user font request overrides this default. ## Impeccable methods adapted for classroom review

Apply this method after each selected variant's six-field draft is complete, as the existing visual review before handoff. Review against the approved outline, teaching purpose, selected visual basis and `classroom-readability.md`. For a scoped revision, review the changed pages and affected relationships. Reuse this review in `quality-gates.md` rather than opening a duplicate audit. The adaptation is self-contained and uses the current agent/tools, without requiring an Impeccable installation, detector, hooks, web server, separate skill invocation or upstream access during production.

Use the four lenses on the actual target available at this stage.

- **Typography / typeset.** Distinguish title, teaching material, task and emphasis by role, size, weight, color and spacing. Keep equivalent roles consistent within the variant. Check long titles, full quotations and essential diagram labels against classroom size floors, line breaks and reading-region capacity. Ordinary-mode font selection and the scoped anti-garbled rules still apply. Assign editable copy to native text layers under the existing ownership contract.
- **Arrangement / layout.** Establish the page's teaching focal point and intended reading order. Group related material through proximity, alignment and spacing, separate distinct ideas and keep diagram relations faithful. Assess the usable text area before giving space to illustration. Adjust text/scene balance by page function, while preserving the chosen style's spatial depth and meaningful detail.
- **Design judgment / critique.** Locate the concrete region where focus competes, a group is ambiguous, hierarchy breaks, or scene/layout becomes interchangeable. Explain how it affects students' reading or the current task. Compare adjacent and nearest similar pages for rhythm, and retain effective scene/content decisions. Derive findings from observed issues, not a fixed quota or generic beauty score. A draft supports planning findings; claims about rendered size, image diversity or editability require the actual artifact.
- **Local finish / polish.** Resolve the prioritized issues with focused changes to hierarchy, grouping, alignment, spacing, color roles, reading-region texture or obstructing details. Apply related repairs together before checking the page again. Map revised decisions into the relevant six fields and, when actual courseware is requested, its corresponding objects/assets. Preserve text, page count, instructional order, diagram meaning, the selected visual identity and palette richness. Retain effective, source-grounded scene decisions, and rebuild a concept identified as generic, repeated or content-incompatible under the existing variation rules. Use the established material-conflict handling when layout cannot satisfy frozen content and the size floor together.

Keep a compact finding in the existing checks/page record with artifact/variant, page/region, observed problem, effect on learning, repair and recheck evidence. ## Per-page visual blueprint

After a direction is selected internally or by prior task confirmation, define every page using this exact user-facing format. Do not add or rename fields. Any internal analysis may remain internal and must not be printed in the blueprint.

### Master-world lock with controlled chapter dialects

The selected direction becomes the deck's only master visual world. Apply the same underlying material/texture logic, palette roles, typographic hierarchy and color roles, icon and border/shape grammar, recurring anchors, and tone to every page. Allow content-driven chapter dialects in related accent palette, brush treatment, scene vocabulary, composition, crop, element choice, information density, and shot language, especially when one deck contains several poems, texts, places, or periods. Do not introduce an unrelated second style such as switching from Eastern literary illustration to neon flat graphics, from documentary photography to cartoon fantasy, or from handmade print to generic glossy 3D. Every variation must still look authored inside the same master world.

Before finalizing the blueprint, compare all pages against the locked master world and flag any page whose `画面风格` introduces an unrelated language or lacks the compact master anchor. Rewrite that page inside the master world; preserve justified chapter dialects rather than flattening every text into identical scenery. This check is internal and does not add a field to the user-facing blueprint.

Consistency applies to the visual language, not to repeated backgrounds or repeated composition. Do not reuse the same complete background, hero image, crop, or scene arrangement across multiple pages. Changing copy/color, shifting ornament, mirroring or cropping the same reference scene does not constitute a new page concept. Track each page's source-grounded focal subject/action, viewpoint, spatial depth and information structure, and compare the whole sequence with both adjacent and nearest similar pages. As an interchangeability check, ask whether the scene/layout remains equally usable for an unrelated page after only swapping the title and text; if so, replace the generic concept with a content-specific one. Related pages may retain the same real place, people and objects, but should reveal the current material, action or relationship rather than inventing new facts for variety. Recurrent palette, brushwork, border grammar, paper texture or small motifs unify the deck; the six fields must still describe a distinct, teachable visual decision for each page.
